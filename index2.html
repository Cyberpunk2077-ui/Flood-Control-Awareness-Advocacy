The issue

Why should we care?

Flooding can destroy homes, interrupt livelihoods, displace communities, and put lives at risk. Public funds for flood control are intended to reduce these risks. That makes transparency, proper construction, inspection, and accountability essential.

Six key findings

Follow the evidence.

01

Thousands of projects, billions in funding

Between July 2022 and May 2025, 9,855 flood-control projects worth more than ₱545 billion were funded. Advocacy matters because incomplete, substandard, or nonexistent projects can leave communities vulnerable while the public deserves transparency about where funds go.

02

Ghost projects and accountability

The DOJ identified 14 ghost projects and reported that work on arrest warrants was underway. When public funds are diverted to projects that do not exist, communities lose both the intended infrastructure and the economic value that the spending was supposed to create.

03

Flooding still affects communities

Reports from Central Luzon described flood-control measures as ineffective during severe flooding. The issue demonstrates why projects need to be properly designed, completed, maintained, and independently checked.

Flooding can cause property damage, disrupt livelihoods, displace families, and threaten lives. Flood risk is not simply something communities should be expected to endure.

04

Inspection reveals missing projects

Reports said around 30,000 projects had been proposed, while only about 10,000 had undergone professional inspection at a reported stage of the investigation. Among an initial 8,000 inspected, 421 were reported as ghost projects. A later report cited 673 ghost projects among 2,000 checked.

One reported estimate placed possible losses at about ₱180 billion.

05

The cost goes beyond infrastructure

Finance Secretary Ralph Recto, citing Department of Finance data, said anomalous flood-control projects may have cost the Philippine economy an estimated ₱42.3 billion to ₱118.5 billion from 2023 to 2025. The reported equivalent was approximately 95,000 to 266,000 potential jobs.

06

Awareness is a public responsibility

Reported estimates of economic losses show that corruption and weak project oversight can affect services and quality of life. Farmers, workers, indigenous communities, and other vulnerable groups can be affected when public resources do not deliver their intended benefits.

Citizens have a legitimate interest in knowing how public money is spent and whether promised public services are delivered.

What advocacy can demand

Build trust through transparency.

🔎 Verify

Projects should be documented, inspected, and verifiably completed before the public considers them successful.

📊 Disclose

Citizens should be able to understand where flood-control budgets go, what was built, and how projects performed.

🏗️ Build properly

Infrastructure must be designed, constructed, and maintained to serve the communities it was funded to protect.

⚖️ Demand accountability

Allegations and findings should be investigated through proper legal and institutional processes, with responsibility established through evidence.
